calm-down — Stop. Think. Act.

A two-mode work-structuring skill. It interviews you, then writes a structured document that turns a rough idea — or a wave of panic — into work someone can actually execute.

2026-06-13

What it does

calm-down sits between “I have a vague pile of work” and “the work is being done well.” Instead of letting you (or a teammate) dive in half-blind, it runs a short interview and produces a single structured document.

It has two modes for the two ways that gap shows up: handing work off to someone else, and steering yourself through work that is already on fire.

The principle

Both a cold handoff and a panicked sprint fail for the same reason: acting before the work is understood. A teammate gets a one-line ticket and guesses. A person under deadline opens fifteen tabs and does the loudest task first. calm-down inserts one deliberate pause and walks through three moves.

01 Stop

Interrupt the reflex to start typing. Name the task and the time you actually have. The pause is reframed as a speed-up, not a delay.

02 Think

Get grilled — one question at a time, each with a recommended answer — until the work is decomposed and understood. Vague answers get probed, not accepted.

03 Act

Generate the document and execute from clarity. The thinking is now on paper, not rattling around in your head.

Two modes

One principle, two situations. You pick the mode by what you're trying to do.

calm-down assign

Hand work off

Produces a handoff document a teammate — or an AI agent — can pick up cold, with zero prior context. Background, current state, suggested assignee, and a per-task breakdown of input, output, checklist, scope, and gotchas.

Output → Handoff document
calm-down triage

Calm yourself down

A calming grill for urgent work you're about to do yourself. It decomposes the panic, makes the hard prioritization call, and hands you a battle plan with time estimates and an opinionated approach per task.

Output → Battle plan

How it works

Both modes share the same four-beat loop. It never asks you to write the task list from scratch — it proposes one and makes you react.

  1. Propose the breakdown. Read your brief, guess the tasks, and confirm before going further.
  2. Grill, one question at a time. Each question carries a recommended answer; ambiguous answers get challenged. It stops the moment the document would be self-sufficient.
  3. Pick a format. Markdown or HTML (this paper theme).
  4. Generate immediately. No preview step — the document is written and saved, and you're told the exact path.

The triage call

Triage mode borrows the engineering discipline of triage — deciding what gets treated now, what waits, and what you walk away from. Every task lands in exactly one bucket:

P0

Must ship. If this slips, the whole thing fails. These come first.

P1

Should ship. Real value, but droppable if time runs out — without failing.

CUT

Not this run. Explicitly named and set down.

Why the cut list calms

Most panic is just unsorted scope. Naming what you are not doing is the move that settles it — it gives you permission to stop carrying everything at once. If everything is still P0, the triage hasn't happened yet.

What you get

A saved document under docs/, in Markdown or in this quiet, paper-like HTML theme — structure carried by hairline borders and one blue accent, never decoration. The flow, end to end:

brief  →  propose tasks  →  grill (Q & A)  →  [triage: P0 / P1 / cut]  →  generate  →  docs/yyyy-mm-dd-topic.{md,html}
