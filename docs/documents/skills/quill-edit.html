quill-edit

Give it your text and get it back in correct British English, tighter and clearer, with a short note on what changed.

skills/productivity/quill-edit British English

What it does

quill-edit proofreads and rephrases English text. It applies British English conventions and the principles of three writing guides:

  • Gary Provost, 100 Ways to Improve Your Writing: rhythm, active voice, concrete language, no filler.
  • Kay Sayce, What Not to Write: common errors, clarity, precise word choice.
  • Craig Shrive, Smashing Grammar: grammar, punctuation and British usage.

You get the full edited text, then Editor's Notes: a short list of the main changes, grouped by category. The skill keeps your meaning. If a passage is unclear, it flags it and does not guess.

How it works

The skill follows five steps. Only the first and the last talk to you before the edit.

  1. Understand the intent. If the purpose is not obvious, the skill asks: professional, academic, casual, or something else? The answer sets the tone.
  2. Analyse. It silently checks grammar, punctuation, spelling, style, rhythm and clarity.
  3. Edit. It returns the full corrected text, then the Editor's Notes. The notes flag patterns, not every instance.
  4. Offer alternatives (optional). If a passage has more than one good phrasing, it gives one or two, marked Alternative.
  5. Invite revision. It asks if you want a different tone, a simpler version, or another look at a passage.

British English conventions

  • Spelling: -ise, -our, -re, -ogue, -ence. Also grey, travelled, programme (but program for software).
  • Punctuation: single quotes first, double quotes inside them. Punctuation goes outside the closing quote unless it is part of the quote. The Oxford comma only when it helps clarity.
  • Dashes: an en dash for ranges (2019–2023) and, with spaces, for asides. Em dashes sparingly.
  • Grammar: collective nouns take a plural verb (the team are ready). Also at the weekend and in hospital.

Style principles

  • Clarity: main point early, active voice, concrete nouns, no throat-clearing openers, strong verbs (decide, not make a decision).
  • Economy: cut filler (due to the fact that → because), redundancy and hedging words.
  • Rhythm: vary sentence length and use parallel structure in lists.
  • Precision: fewer or less, that or which, clear pronouns, and confused pairs such as practise and practice.
  • Tone: match formality to purpose and replace jargon and corporate fog with plain words.

The skill replies in chat. It does not write a file. For the input “We need to make a decision on the color of the new program due to the fact that the team is behind.”, a reply looks like this:

---
**Edited text**

We need to decide on the colour of the new programme because the team are behind.

---
**Editor's Notes**

- **Grammar:** Collective noun takes a plural verb ('the team are').
- **Spelling:** color → colour; program → programme (not software).
- **Style:** 'make a decision' → 'decide'; 'due to the fact that' → 'because'.
- **Rhythm:** No structural change needed.

Common questions

Will it change what I mean?
No. If a passage is unclear, the skill flags it and asks. It does not guess at your meaning.
Will it remove a fragment I wrote on purpose?
No. It keeps deliberate stylistic choices and mentions them in the notes instead of correcting them.
What if my text is already good?
The skill says so plainly. Then it points to one or two small refinements and does not invent problems.
Will it reorder or cut sections of a long text?
Only after it asks you. Structural changes to longer texts need your approval first.
Does it force the Oxford comma?
No. In British English it is optional, so the skill uses it only when it makes a sentence clearer.

It’s working if

  • All spelling in the edit is British, for example organise, colour and centre.
  • The Editor's Notes list patterns in a few lines, not every single change.
  • The edit is shorter or clearer, and it still says what you meant.
  • The tone matches the purpose you gave: formal stays formal, casual stays casual.
  • The skill asks before it changes the structure of a long text.

Where it fits

quill-edit is the last step before you send or publish text. Use it after the content is complete.

draft text → quill-edit → edited text + Editor's Notes → adjust tone? → send / publish

To make the first draft from a conversation or from code, use write-doc. To shape a prompt for an AI rather than prose for people, use revise-prompt.
