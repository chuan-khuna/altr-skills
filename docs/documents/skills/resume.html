resume

Give it a bullet, an entry or a whole resume. You get back text a recruiter reads in one pass, and that you can defend line by line in an interview.

skills/writing/resume British English manual only

What it does

resume rewrites and proofreads the resume text you give it. You can give a single bullet, an entry, a summary, a whole resume, or rough notes about your work. You get the text back, ready to paste.

The rules come from three sources, in this order of precedence:

  1. The skill's own resume rules. The content rules come mainly from Harvard FAS Career Services, “Create a strong resume”. The grammar rules come mainly from the Guardian, “Grammar rules for your CV: top tips”.
  2. The technical-writing and unslop skills.
  3. House style: British English, the grammar of Craig Shrives' Smashing Grammar, and the prose habits of Gary Provost's 100 Ways to Improve Your Writing.

The skill does not invent achievements. Every claim in the result is traceable to your text or your answers. If a bullet needs a number you did not give, the skill keeps the bullet without it and asks you.

Prerequisites

The skill reads the technical-writing and unslop skills in full before it edits. Both are in the same writing group, so the altr-skills plugin installs them together. If you copy resume on its own, copy the other two as well.

How it works

  1. Read the rules. The skill reads the technical-writing and unslop skills in full.
  2. Tailor, if you give a job description. The skill lists each requirement and sorts it as Direct (your text shows it), Bridge (your text shows a close equivalent) or Gap (your text does not show it). It writes a plan and stops. It rewrites only after you approve the plan.
  3. Rewrite. Each bullet gets one result: the action, the thing it acted on, and the result. The skill uses your numbers. Where you gave no number, it keeps the bullet and adds a question.
  4. Proofread. The skill checks grammar and British spelling sentence by sentence. It checks homophones such as practice and practise by meaning, because a spellchecker passes them.
  5. Check. It checks the text against every resume rule and every unslop rule by number. The step is done when no line fails a rule.
  6. Return. You get the text, then a list of changes that altered a fact or dropped a claim, then the questions.

Content rules

  • One result per bullet. Quantify with your number. With no number, give the scale: how many sites, users or teams.
  • Plain action verbs. Built, designed, led, analysed, deployed, automated.
  • Ownership. “Built” or “led” for work you did alone or led. “Co-developed” or “contributed to” for shared work. If the text does not say, the skill keeps your verb and asks.
  • Evidence, not adjectives. “Passionate” and “results-driven” become the fact that shows them.
  • Specific nouns. “Detect vehicles on 12 CCTV feeds with YOLO”, not “work on computer vision solutions”.
  • Length. 30 words or fewer per bullet, most relevant bullet first.

Person and tense

  • Bullets have no subject and no pronoun. The verb starts the line.
  • Summaries and profiles use “I” from the first sentence to the last.
  • Present tense for the current role. Past tense for every other entry.

What it writes

The skill replies in chat. It does not write a file.

PartContent
Tailoring plan (only with a job description)Entries to keep, bullets to reorder or rewrite, and the job's terms each change uses
Rewritten textYour resume text, ready to paste
Changes listEach change that altered a fact or dropped a claim
QuestionsMissing numbers, unclear ownership, and gaps against the job description

For the input “Passionate engineer who worked on computer vision solutions for traffic.”, a reply can look like this:

- Built a vehicle detection pipeline with YOLO for CCTV traffic feeds.

Changes
- Removed "passionate": the bullet now shows the work instead.
- Changed "worked on" to "built": please confirm you built it alone.

Questions
1. How many camera feeds did the pipeline cover?
2. Did detection accuracy or review time improve? By how much?

Common questions

Will it make up numbers to make a bullet stronger?
No. Every claim comes from your text or your answers. A bullet with no number stays without one, and you get a question.
Will it copy keywords from the job description?
Only where your text supports them. For a Bridge, it names what you did, not what the job asked for. A Gap becomes a question.
Does it use American spelling for a US job?
No. The skill writes British English. It keeps the maker's spelling only in product names and code, such as the CSS property color.
Why did it write “APIs” and not “API's”?
Plurals, including acronym plurals, take no apostrophe. That is one of the Guardian grammar rules the skill uses.
Can I reject a change?
Yes. You stay the author. The changes list makes each change easy to accept or reject.

It’s working if

  • Every bullet starts with a plain verb and states one result.
  • No bullet holds a number, tool or result that you did not give.
  • Spelling is British: analysed, optimisation, modelling.
  • With a job description, the skill shows a plan and waits before it rewrites.
  • The reply ends with the changes list and the questions.

Where it fits

resume sits on top of the two general writing skills. It reads them first, then applies its own rules over them.

notes or old resume → resume (reads technical-writing + unslop) → text + changes + questions → your answers → resume again
